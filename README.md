Bearnie is a simple, interactive desktop pet that I built entirely from scratch in just 10 hours. This project was created specifically as a submission for the Hack Club "Playground" event, which officially wrapped up on October 12, 2026.

## Compatibility & Installation

Right now, Bearnie runs natively on Linux and Windows operating systems. Unfortunately, macOS is not supported at this time.

If you want to download Bearnie and try him out on your own desktop, here is exactly how to do it:

Navigate to the folder named "Builds" (Make sure it is the plural one/Builds, NOT the folder named "BUILD").

Download the .zip file located inside.

Once it finishes downloading, unzip the folder to your machine. Inside, you will find the specific version of Bearnie that matches your operating system.

## What can Bearnie do?
Bearnie is fully interactive and has a bunch of different mechanics to mess around with while you work:

## Petting & Menus 
If you left-click on Bearnie, you can give him a pet. If you right-click him, it opens up a context menu where you can choose to feed him, force him to take a nap, let him do a little dance, or open his toy box.

## The Toy Box 
Bearnie comes equipped with four specific items: a bouncy ball, a rubber duck, a pot of honey, and a pogo stick.

## Physics & Interactions 
You can actually throw the ball at Bearnie, and it features a very bouncy physics system. The rubber ducky can also be thrown, though it is slightly less bouncy than the ball. If you pull out the pogo stick, Bearnie will jump on it and bounce around. You can also feed him the honey directly by throwing him a jar, either leaving it on your desktop or giving it directly to him. You can also feed him files! (WON'T AFFECT FILES).You can also slap him if you move your cursor really fast in his direction.

## Cursor Attacks 
Bearnie actively tracks you. He will follow your mouse cursor with his eyes as you move it across the screen. Occasionally, he will charge and physically jump onto your cursor. He usually hangs on for about 10 to 14 seconds before jumping off on his own, but if you need your mouse back immediately, you can aggressively shake the cursor to throw him off.

## Window Dragging 
If Bearnie is blocking something important on your screen, you can click, hold, and throw him across your desktop to get him out of the way.

## Dynamic Time & Activity
Bearnie is programmed to be slightly aware of your actual environment and schedule:

## Nighttime Naps 
He has a pretty cool dynamic feature tied to your system clock. Between the hours of 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM, Bearnie gets tired and has a significantly higher chance of randomly taking naps on your screen.

## Absence Detection 
Bearnie knows when you leave. If your computer is inactive for 6 or more hours, he will be noticeably very happy to see you when you finally come back and move the mouse.
